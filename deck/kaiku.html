The problem

A robot learns a job by watching a person do it.

Sorting parts took our robot 128 demonstrations to get to 92%.
Every one of them is a person in gloves, at one robot, at one table.

Sort the parts once.

47 more robots sort theirs at the same moment, each at a table of its own.

Kaiku is Finnish for echo

It is not a copy.

  1. Every table is different. The parts lie somewhere else, turned another way, sometimes another shape.
  2. Each robot redoes your motion around its own parts, while you are still moving.
  3. Physics checks every robot, and so does the challenge's safety filter. Only the clean runs are saved, in the challenge's dataset format.

16 demonstrations: 0% alone, 81% with echoes.

32 with echoes beat 128 without. The operator wears the gloves a quarter of the time.

Share of 96 scenes nobody had seen in which every part ended in its own bin. Mean of 3 training runs.

Then the gloves come off.

The policy trained on the echoes sorts tables it has never seen. 48 robots, nobody driving.

What is real

Built on the challenge's own parts.

  1. Rokoko's driver and hand solver read the gloves. Kaiku takes their stream as it is.
  2. The G1 gets its real commands. The challenge's own runner took 1,800 in a row through its safety filter, none clipped.
  3. The data is the challenge's format, LeRobot v3.0 and GR00T, ready to train on.
  4. Not yet: a real G1. Everything runs in a physics twin of the sorting station.
